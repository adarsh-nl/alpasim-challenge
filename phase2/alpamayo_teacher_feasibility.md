# Alpamayo 2 Super — teacher feasibility

Read-only, CPU, no weights downloaded, no GPU job. Evidence from the HF API, the
published `LICENSE`, and `config.json`.

**Recommendation: VIABLE as a tail-specialist trajectory teacher** — with a
throughput caveat that makes it a supplement to Diffusion-Planner, not a
replacement. DP stays the main line.

---

## 1. Licence — PASSES §5, and more cleanly than Diffusion-Planner

`nvidia/Alpamayo2-Super`: `license: openmdw-1.1`, **ungated**, `LICENSE` file
present in the repo. Applying the code-vs-weights distinction from
`phase2/dp_license.md`, this is the *weights* licence — the artifact we would
actually use.

OpenMDW-1.1, quoted from the repo's own `LICENSE`:

> "permission is hereby granted, free of charge, to deal in the Model Materials
> **without restriction**, including under all copyright, patent, database, and
> trade secret rights included or embodied therein."

> "If you distribute any portion of the Model Materials, you shall retain in your
> distribution (1) a copy of this agreement, and (2) all copyright notices…"

And the clause that settles distillation:

> **"This agreement does not impose any restrictions or obligations with respect
> to any use, modification, or sharing of any *outputs* generated by using the
> Model Materials."**

So:

- **Teacher outputs are explicitly unencumbered.** A student trained on Alpamayo
  labels carries no obligation from this licence. §5's Apache-2.0-to-the-public
  requirement is satisfied with nothing to negotiate.
- The retention obligation attaches only to distributing the *Model Materials*.
  The teacher is offline and never ships, so it does not bite. If we ever did
  redistribute weights, we would carry the licence text.
- One real term to note: **patent-retaliation termination** — suing NVIDIA (or
  anyone) for infringement over the Model Materials terminates the grant. Not a
  practical constraint for us, and worth reading alongside terms §6(c), where we
  have already covenanted not to assert IP claims against NVIDIA anyway.

**This is a strictly better licence position than Diffusion-Planner**, whose code
repo has no LICENSE at all and whose `setup.py` self-contradicts. Alpamayo's
grant is explicit, in-repo, and output-permissive.

## 2. Memory — does not fit one L40S in bf16; 2-way sharding is the clean answer

From `config.json` and the file manifest:

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| `dtype` | `bfloat16` |
| Weight shards | 15 × safetensors |
| Weights footprint | **~68.4 GB** (repo total 71.64 GB) |
| Implied parameters | **~34 B** (68.4 GB ÷ 2 bytes) |
| Architecture | `Alpamayo2Super` — VLM + action "expert" |

Against an L40S at 48 GiB:

| Strategy | Weights | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Single L40S, bf16 | 68.4 GB | **does not fit** |
| **2× L40S, bf16, sharded** | 34.2 GB/card | **fits, with ~13 GB/card for activations + KV cache** |
| 4× L40S, bf16 | 17.1 GB/card | comfortable; `hpc-node11/12` have 4× |
| Single L40S, 8-bit | ~34 GB | fits, ~14 GB headroom |
| Single L40S, 4-bit | ~17 GB | comfortable |

**Recommendation: 2× or 4× L40S in bf16, not quantisation.** The charter's
caution is the right one and I want to put it strongly: we would be using this
model *specifically* for long-tail judgment on the scenes where our geometric
driver fails. Quantisation degrades exactly the low-probability, high-consequence
reasoning we are buying. Since `ps` has 4× L40S nodes, bf16 sharding is
available and there is no reason to accept that risk. Quantise only if the
multi-GPU path proves unworkable, and if you do, validate on the two known zeros
before trusting it.

Unmeasured: activation + KV-cache footprint for trace generation. It depends on
context length and number of flow-matching integration steps, neither of which I
can infer reliably. With ~13 GB/card spare at 2-way this is likely fine, but it
is the number that would decide 2-way vs 4-way and it needs a smoke test.

## 3. Output shape — it emits trajectories directly. This is the key finding.

The charter flagged a risk that Alpamayo might produce only reasoning text, which
would demote it to a gating-signal source. **It does not.** From `config.json`:

```json
"action_space_cfg": {
  "_target_": "alpamayo2_super.action_space.UnicycleAccelCurvatureActionSpace",
  "n_waypoints": 64,
  "dt": 0.1,
  "accel_bounds":     [-9.8, 9.8],
  "curvature_bounds": [-0.33, 0.33]
},
"diffusion_cfg": {
  "_target_": "alpamayo2_super.diffusion.flow_matching.FlowMatching",
  "int_method": "euler",
  "inference_guidance_weight": 3.0
}
```

- **64 waypoints at dt = 0.1 → a 6.4 s trajectory**, produced by a flow-matching
  diffusion head over a unicycle accel/curvature action space.
- Action bounds are physical (±9.8 m/s², ±0.33 m⁻¹ curvature), so outputs are
  **kinematically constrained by construction** — which is directly relevant to
  D1's far-horizon feasibility audit, where Diffusion-Planner's 8 s labels are
  unvalidatable extrapolation.

Two consequences:

1. It is usable as a **drop-in trajectory teacher**. Its 6.4 s horizon is shorter
   than DP's 8 s but *longer* than the ~2 s we can validate against GT — and
   unlike DP it is bounded by an explicit action space rather than free-form
   regression.
2. It can plausibly serve **both** roles — trajectory labels *and* an
   interpretable reasoning signal for information-gating — which was the hoped-for
   best case.

## 4. Throughput — the real constraint, and it is estimated, not measured

I cannot measure this without running the model, and no per-sample figure is
published that I could verify. What can be bounded:

- ~34 B parameters, bf16, sharded across 2 cards with inter-GPU traffic.
- Flow-matching inference is **iterative** (Euler integration over N steps), so
  cost is roughly N × a 34 B forward, not one forward.
- VLM prefill over multi-camera context adds materially.

Order-of-magnitude, stated as an assumption rather than a result: **~1–3 s per
sample** on 2× L40S. Against the re-targeted set:

| Set | Samples | @2 s/sample | vs DP @8.3/s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,491 renderable scenes × 8 | ~11,928 | **~6.6 h wall (≈13 GPU-h on 2 cards)** | DP: ~24 min |
| Two known zeros + 18 tail scenes × 8 | 160 | **~5 min** | trivial |

So Alpamayo is roughly **15–30× slower than Diffusion-Planner**. That settles its
role: it is not the bulk labeller. It is worth spending on the **tail** — the
scenes where DP is least trustworthy and where our zeros actually live.

Caveat worth stating plainly: the 1–3 s estimate could be off by several×
(diffusion step count is configurable and I do not know the default; a 34 B model
sharded over 2 cards may be bandwidth-bound). The smoke test below exists to
replace this estimate with a measurement before any bulk spend.

## 5. Recommendation

**Viable as a tail-specialist teacher.** Concretely:

- Licence: clean, better than DP. No blocker.
- Memory: fits 2× L40S bf16. Do not quantise a reasoning model we are buying for
  long-tail judgment.
- Output: real 6.4 s trajectories with physical action bounds — a genuine
  teacher, not just a text source.
- Throughput: too slow for the full set; right for the tail.

Proposed division of labour: **DP labels the bulk; Alpamayo labels the tail and
supplies the map-dependence / reasoning signal for information-gating.** That
plays to each model's strength and keeps DP as the main line, as instructed.

Note this is orthogonal to the render-set blocker in
`reports/d2_render_set_is_the_eval_set.md`: Alpamayo labels derive from the
trajdata cache, so they are unaffected by whether we may train on navtest
renders. Its feasibility stands either way.

## 6. Staged smoke test — 20 scenes, NOT run

`rl/alpamayo_smoke.sbatch` (staged). Targets the two known zeros plus 18
renderable tail scenes, to answer the three things this report had to estimate:

1. real sec/sample with traces (replaces §4's estimate),
2. peak VRAM per card at 2-way sharding (replaces §2's unmeasured activation
   figure),
3. whether its trajectories differ usefully from DP's on `dd162898` and
   `8cc8fe63` — i.e. whether it would actually have avoided our two zeros.

If (3) comes back "same answer as DP", the tail-specialist thesis is dead and
Alpamayo reduces to a reasoning-signal source. **That is the single most
informative 5 minutes of GPU on this whole thread**, which is why the smoke test
is scoped to it rather than to throughput alone.
